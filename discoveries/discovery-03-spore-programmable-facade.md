# Discovery 03 — Programmable Bacterial Spore Actuators for Zero-Energy Facades

## Status: Grounded in peer-reviewed science, not yet applied in architecture at panel scale
## Evidence level: L2 in material science, L0 in architecture

---

## The gap in current knowledge

Hygromorphic wood bilayers are the most architecturally mature passive actuator, validated at facade scale (Nature Communications, 2024). However they have a key limitation: response time for thicker materials is hours to days.

Bacterial spore actuators (Bacillus subtilis on latex substrate) have been validated at material scale with:
- Response time: less than 3 minutes (MDPI Sustainability, 2021)
- Reversibility: confirmed over 10 cycles in this study; comparable systems confirmed at 1 million cycles without performance change (Chen et al., referenced in MDPI 2021)
- Lift capacity: 150% of own mass
- Programmable deflection: number of spore monolayers determines deflection angle
- Durability: spores viable for hundreds to thousands of years in dormancy
- Zero energy input required

Cork substrates tested for spore adhesion in 2025 (Tocco.Earth, October 2025): 50+ substrates tested, cork identified as optimal for stability and cellular structure. Cork is also a regenerative, Australian-sourceable material.

## The original contribution

This project proposes **Programmable Spore Facade Panels** as a zero-energy architectural actuator system:

### Concept

Each panel in the facade has a bilayer actuator strip made from:
- Substrate: cork sheet (3–5mm) for structural backing and optimal spore adhesion
- Active layer: Bacillus subtilis spore monolayers (4 layers = maximum deflection)
- Thickness: 0.5–2mm active layer
- Response: passive deflection in response to ambient humidity change

### Programmable behaviour

- 1 spore monolayer = small deflection angle (15°–20°)
- 2 monolayers = intermediate deflection (25°–32°)
- 4 monolayers = maximum deflection (43°)
- Different panels in the same facade can have different monolayer counts
- This creates a **programmable spatial response gradient** without electronics or sensors

### Information analogy connection

A facade with programmed spatial gradient of spore monolayer counts will produce a spatially differentiated humidity response: panels in humid micro-zones (e.g. near a courtyard or green wall) open more than panels in dry zones. This creates a non-zero TFE (Discovery 01) driven entirely by passive material programming, with zero control hardware.

---

## Falsifiable claims

1. Cork-spore bilayer panels (150mm x 300mm) will deflect measurably within 3 minutes of a 30% RH change. (Testable in a desktop humidity chamber.)
2. Panels with different monolayer counts will deflect to measurably different angles at the same RH. (Testable with protractor and time-lapse camera.)
3. After 100 wet-dry cycles, deflection angles remain within 10% of initial values. (Testable over 1–2 weeks of daily testing.)

---

## Why this is hidden from the mainstream

Bacterial spore actuators are published in sustainability and microbiology journals, not architectural journals. The 2021 MDPI paper describes architectural potential but does not prototype at panel scale. The 2025 Tocco.Earth work demonstrates passive sensing, not actuation at facade scale.

The connection between programmable monolayer count (material science parameter) and spatially differentiated facade entropy (architectural performance metric) has not been made in any reviewed paper.
